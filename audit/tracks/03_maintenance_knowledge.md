## 1. InterNACHI Residential Standards of Practice
- https://www.nachi.org/sop.htm — private-association inspection standard (not a code).
- Supports: non-invasive visual inspection of accessible areas (§1.1); systems covered (§3); exclusions (§2.2: mold, radon, lead, asbestos, pests, code compliance, cost estimates, underground items). Key: does not determine life expectancy of components (§2.1.08); roof service-life prediction excluded (§3.1); water heater life expectancy excluded (§3.6). §1.2: a system near end of life is not by itself a material defect.
- Does NOT establish: any maintenance interval, life expectancy, or cost.
- Copyright: © 2006–2026 InterNACHI. Terms of Use (https://www.nachi.org/termsofuse.htm §2): no reproduction/commercial use without written consent; personal informational use with attribution. Government free-use license applies only to government entities. → Link only + short attributed paraphrase of scope.
- Rule categories: "what a home inspection does/doesn't cover" explainer; disclaimer language for life-expectancy rules.

## 2a. InterNACHI Standard Estimated Life Expectancy Chart
- https://www.nachi.org/life-expectancy.htm — estimate table.
- Supports: ranges e.g. furnace 15–25 yr; conventional water heater 6–12 yr; asphalt 3-tab 20 yr / architectural 30 yr; sump pump 7 yr; copper water lines 50–70 yr; cast-iron waste above ground 60 yr, below ground 50–60 yr. Caveats on page: guidelines only, not guarantees; assumes regular maintenance and normal wear; varies with usage, weather, installation, maintenance, materials.
- Does NOT establish: failure prediction, replacement trigger, maintenance interval; methodology not cited.
- Copyright: © InterNACHI; same Terms → link only; cite ranges as "InterNACHI estimate" with caveat; do not reproduce table.
- Rule categories: age-based "plan for replacement window" (planning-assumption label), never "will fail."

## 4. US DOE Energy Saver
- Copyright: DOE Web Policies (https://www.energy.gov/about-us/web-policies): government information in the public domain; may be copied with acknowledgment; third-party content may be copyrighted. → quote/paraphrase with attribution.
- 4a Furnaces and Boilers — https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/furnaces-and-boilers (alias https://www.energy.gov/node/374305). - 4b Maintaining Your Air Conditioner — https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/maintaining-your-air-conditioner. - 4c Storage Water Heaters — https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/storage-water-heaters (alias https://www.energy.gov/node/1266071). Supports: flush a quart every three months; check T&P valve every six months; inspect anode rod every three to four years.

- 4e Air sealing — https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/do-it-yourself-savings-project-seal-air-leaks-caulk. Supports: caulk windows/doors/sill; leave duct sealing to contractor. No interval.

## 6. CPSC / USFA / NFPA — alarms, CO, dryer, GFCI
- CPSC publications: government publications, public domain (17 U.S.C. §105; CPSC clearance directive).
- 6a CPSC Pub 559 "Smoke Alarms – Why, Where, and Which" — https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/559.pdf. Supports: test monthly; replace alarms older than 10 years; replace batteries at least annually.
- 6b USFA Smoke Alarms — https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prepare-for-fire/smoke-alarms/. Supports: test monthly; replace entire alarm every 10 years; batteries at least yearly.
- 6c CPSC NSN-9 CO — https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/COSafety_0.pdf. Supports: professional inspection of all fuel-burning heating systems every year (water heaters, furnaces named); CO alarms near bedrooms; batteries monthly check/annual replace.
- 6d CPSC CO Fact Sheet — https://www.cpsc.gov/safety-education/safety-guides/carbon-monoxide/carbon-monoxide-fact-sheet. Supports: heating system professionally inspected and serviced annually; check chimneys and flues for blockages; alarms have a manufacturer replacement age (no number → from label).
- 6e CPSC Pub 5022 dryers — https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/5022.pdf. Supports: clean lint screen each load; clean vent/exhaust duct periodically (no interval); replace plastic/foil duct with rigid/semi-rigid metal.
- 6f CPSC Pub 099 GFCI — https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/099_0.pdf; Pub 513 https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/513.pdf. Supports: test GFCIs at least monthly, after installation and power failures; GFCI may replace ungrounded two-slot receptacles with "NO EQUIPMENT GROUND" label.
- 6g NFPA 72 / 54 / 211: paid; tip sheets robots-blocked; © NFPA. → Do not rely on NFPA for the prototype; CPSC/USFA state the same rules in the public domain.
- Rule categories: monthly alarm/GFCI tests; annual battery; 10-yr smoke-alarm replacement; CO-alarm replacement per label; annual pro inspection of fuel-burning heating; dryer vent.
